How Slip works

One question, a few friends, sealed picks.

Everyone commits to a pick before anyone can see. The seal is a zero-knowledge proof made on your own iPhone, so nobody, including us, has to be trusted not to peek. At the deadline, each opening is checked against its seal.

Built in Wave 1: one local round, proved end to end on the phone Planned: connected crews and network submission

The flow, as the app shows it

Screens are the app's own renders, not mock-ups.
1

Pick in private

A question, two sides, a deadline. You choose on your own phone. The pick is the circuit's witness, and its salt is derived in-circuit from a secret that never leaves the device. Nothing is typed into a server.

Leaves the phonenothing
2

Hold to seal

Sealing is a 1.2 second hold, not a tap, because a seal cannot be taken back. While you hold, the phone runs the sealPick circuit and generates the proof itself. There is no proof server in this path.

Proved on the phone
Becomes publiccommitment · proof · member id hash
3

The ticket

What you keep is a receipt: the wax seal, when it was made, and the proof's own numbers. Your side is concealed behind a hold-to-peek. In the demo run the seal proof took 0.998 s and weighs 4,480 bytes.

On the ticketProof time 0.998 s · Key load 0.027 s · 4480 bytes
4

Open together

After the deadline the opening window starts for everyone at once. Each opening is proved against the commitment made earlier. A mismatch is rejected by the contract, not smoothed over by the app.

Becomes publicyour side · the opening proof
5

Call it

Opening establishes what was sealed. It does not establish what happened. The outcome is called separately, proved as its own circuit, and only then does anyone score. Wave 1 is unitless: points and banter, no stakes.

Becomes publicthe outcome · the settlement proof
