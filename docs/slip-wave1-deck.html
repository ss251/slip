Sealed group predictions, proved on your phone.

Slip is an iOS app for calling things with friends. You commit to a pick, it stays hidden, and later you prove your opening matches the seal you made — without anyone having to take your word for it.

Midnight Buildathon · Wave 1 · github.com/ss251/slip
01
The problem

Every pick’em app promises nobody peeked.

The promise rests on a server you cannot inspect, run by people you do not know. When the group chat argues about who actually called it, the evidence is a screenshot and a claim.

A prediction is only interesting if it was fixed before the outcome. That is a cryptographic property, and today it is enforced by trust.

02
The loop

Commit. Seal. Open. Prove.

Pick

Choose a side. The choice is witness data; its salt is derived in-circuit from the device secret, never supplied by the host.

Seal

Hold to seal. The phone generates the zero-knowledge proof itself. In this Wave 1 build nothing is submitted — proof and commitment stay on the device.

Open

At the deadline the pick opens and is checked against its commitment by the circuit.

Call

The outcome is settled separately. Opening establishes what was sealed, not what happened.

A reveal that does not match its commitment is rejected by the contract, not smoothed over by the client.

03
What makes it different

The seal proof is generated on the phone, by the phone.

MidnightKit is our Swift package wrapping midnight-zk and midnight-ledger as a Rust static library for aarch64-apple-ios, running the Compact runtime under JavaScriptCore.

  • Removing the proof server is not an optimisation. A proof server is exactly the party that would otherwise have to be trusted.
  • The witness — {choice, salt} — is never logged, never persisted unencrypted, never transmitted. Only the commitment and the proof travel.
  • One Compact contract, six circuits: enrollMember · createSlip · sealPick · reveal · settle · dispute

06
Limits, stated plainly

What this does not claim.

  • Connected crews are not shipped. enrollMember is gated by assertSteward(), so joining is not self-service. Tapping an invite imports public round metadata; it does not establish on-chain membership. The app says so on screen.
  • The deployment is a local devnet, not a public testnet.
  • The proof establishes that an opening matches its seal. It does not establish that nobody saw an unlocked phone.

These are in the README in the same words. A privacy product that overstates itself has already failed at the thing it sells.
